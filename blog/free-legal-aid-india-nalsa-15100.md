# Free Legal Aid in India: Who Is Eligible and How to Apply

Access to justice should not depend on the ability to pay. **Article 39A of the Constitution** and the **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987** make free legal aid a right for eligible people in India.

## Who is eligible? (Section 12)

- Women and children
- Members of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes
- Persons with disabilities
- Victims of trafficking or of disasters
- Industrial workers
- Persons in custody
- Persons with income below the prescribed limit

## What free legal aid can include

- Representation by a panel advocate
- Certified copies of orders and documents
- Legal advice

## How to apply

- **Call the NALSA toll-free helpline: 15100**
- Apply on plain paper to your **District Legal Services Authority (DLSA)**, Taluk or Sub-divisional Legal Services Committee, the High Court Legal Services Committee or the Supreme Court Legal Services Committee
- Or apply online through the **NALSA portal**

## Lok Adalat: no court fee

No court fee is payable when a matter is filed in a **Lok Adalat**, where disputes are settled by mutual agreement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do women need to show an income certificate for free legal aid?**
Women are a listed category under Section 12, separate from the income criterion.

**Is free legal aid only for criminal cases?**
No, it covers civil and other matters too.
