# How to Make a Valid Will in India

Many family property disputes begin with one simple gap: there was no clear will. Making a will (vasiyat) is easier than most people think. Under the **Indian Succession Act, 1925**, a few basic rules decide whether it is valid.

## Write it and sign it before two witnesses

Under **Section 63**, a will must be in writing and **signed by the person making it** (the testator). It must be **attested by two witnesses**, each of whom has seen the testator sign, and both witnesses sign the will too. Plain paper is enough; **no stamp paper is needed** for a will.

## Don't make a beneficiary a witness

Under **Section 67**, if a witness (or the witness's husband or wife) is also given something in the will, **that gift to them is void**. The will itself still stands, but that person loses their share. So choose witnesses who receive nothing under the will.

## Is registration compulsory?

No. An **unregistered will is valid**. Registration under the Registration Act, 1908 is **optional**, done at the Sub-Registrar's office. A registered will is harder to dispute because there is an official record of it.

## You can change or cancel it any time

Under **Section 62**, a will can be **changed or revoked at any time** during your lifetime. If there are several wills, the **latest valid will** counts. Making a fresh will is often clearer than adding many changes to an old one.

## Probate is no longer compulsory

Earlier, Section 213 required probate for certain wills made in or about property in Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata. The **Repealing and Amending Act, 2025** removed that requirement, so probate is **no longer compulsory** anywhere in India.

## Keep these records

- The original signed will, in a safe place that a trusted person knows about
- Names and addresses of both witnesses
- Registration receipt or registered copy, if you registered it
- A list of your property and bank/investment details referred to in the will

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I write my will by hand?**
Yes. A handwritten will is valid if it is signed by you and attested by two witnesses.

**Do I need to register my will?**
No, registration is optional. It only makes the will harder to challenge.

**Can a family member who gets property be a witness?**
They can sign, but the gift to them becomes void. It is safer to choose witnesses who receive nothing.
