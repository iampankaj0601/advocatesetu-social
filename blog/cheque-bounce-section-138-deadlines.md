# Cheque Bounce in India: Section 138 Deadlines

A bounced cheque is not just a banking hiccup. Under **Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881**, dishonour of a cheque for an existing debt can be a criminal offence. But the payee must act within strict timelines.

## The 30 – 15 – 30 rule

| Step | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Send a written demand notice to the drawer | Within **30 days** of receiving the bank's return memo |
| Drawer pays the amount | Within **15 days** of receiving the notice |
| File the complaint if still unpaid | Within **one month** after the 15-day period ends |

## Present the cheque while it is valid

A cheque must be presented within its validity period. Under RBI rules, cheques are **valid for 3 months** from the date written on them.

## Punishment under Section 138

The court can order **imprisonment of up to 2 years**, a **fine of up to twice the cheque amount**, or both.

## Documents to keep

- The original cheque and the bank's return memo
- A copy of the demand notice and proof of delivery
- Any agreement, invoice or messages showing the debt

## Frequently asked questions

**What if I missed the 30-day notice window?**
Missing a deadline can weaken the case. Speak to an advocate about your options.

**Can the matter be settled?**
Yes. If the drawer pays within 15 days of the notice, the matter can end there.
